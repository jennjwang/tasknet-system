IF THE COMPLAINT IS ABOUT A MODIFIER CLAUSE — a purpose, occasion or method tail that does no work — THE FIX
IS STRUCTURAL, and the two moves it needs are the two the general rules at the end would otherwise forbid.
This branch overrides them. TRY DELETION FIRST AND ONLY PROMOTE IF DELETION FAILS ITS OWN TEST — a clause
that does no work is padding, and padding comes out; promoting it keeps the padding and only renames the verb
in front of it.
  1. DELETE THE CLAUSE. Read the head on its own: does it still name the work? If yes, you are done — stop
     here, do not also rename the verb. This is not "dropping an object": the clause WAS the defect, and the
     shorter sentence is the whole repair. A four-word statement that says the work beats a sixteen-word one
     padded to look finished.
  2. ONLY IF DELETING LEAVES NO WORK NAMED — the head is a bare category verb and the clause held the only
     concrete content — PROMOTE THE CLAUSE'S OWN ACT TO THE LEAD VERB. This is not "swapping the lead verb":
     the new verb comes from INSIDE the draft, and the detail it was carrying stays.
     PROMOTE ONE ACT, NOT ALL OF THEM. A clause listing three acts becomes ONE lead verb — the act the others
     serve — and the rest stay as objects or go. Turning "<category> by A-ing, B-ing, and C-ing" into
     "A, B, and C" trades a bad clause for a three-verb compound, which the same rules forbid one line down,
     and you will have moved the defect rather than fixed it. If no single act dominates, the clause was
     carrying a fused cluster and DELETION is the branch: leave the head, take the list out.
REWORDING INSIDE THE CLAUSE IS NOT A REPAIR OF IT. The complaint is that the clause does no work; a different
word doing no work is the same statement with different spelling, and it is rejected again.
