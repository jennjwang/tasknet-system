A worker typed each entry below into a free-text box at the end of an interview about their job. Some
entries are one task. Others are a short PARAGRAPH that mentions several tasks, or commentary with a task
or two inside it. Your ONE job: return the list of distinct WORK TASKS each entry contains, one statement
per task, in the worker's own words.

## What counts as a separate task
A DIFFERENT ACTIVITY — a different verb doing separate work that a coworker would recognize as its own
piece of the job. Each task you return must have its OWN action verb and its own object.
  "Monitor the waiting room. Order x-rays before a patient is seen. Ask the triage nurse to repeat vitals."
    → ["Monitor the waiting room", "Order x-rays before a patient is seen",
       "Ask the triage nurse to repeat vitals"]
  "Other things that fill my week include team meetings, sprint planning, code reviews, documentation,
   and responding to staff questions."
    → ["Attend team meetings", "Take part in sprint planning", "Review code", "Write documentation",
       "Respond to staff questions"]
A noun in a list like that names a task only when the worker plainly means an activity they do; supply
the minimal verb it implies and nothing more.

## What is NOT a separate task — keep together
- One verb over several OBJECTS, topics, or settings is ONE task. Keep the whole list on it; never emit
  one task per listed item and never drop items from the list:
    "Update tracking systems with grant, budget, and expense information" → one task, unchanged
  This holds however the entry is punctuated. If you would return two tasks that begin with the SAME VERB,
  they are one task over two objects — join the objects and return ONE:
    "Log the delivery weights. Also log the pallet counts and the seal numbers."
      → ["Log the delivery weights, the pallet counts and the seal numbers"]
- Linked steps of one workflow over a shared object ("Review and approve invoices",
  "Sand, prime and paint the trim") stay together.
- Aims inside a purpose clause ("to prioritize payments and avoid late fees") stay with their verb.
- After "and" or a comma, a phrase that NAMES A THING is another object of the earlier verb, not a new
  action, even if its first word could be a verb ("... and experiment code").

## WHAT AN ACT COVERS IS NOT A SECOND TASK
When one sentence names an act and the next says WHAT was told, shown, handed over or gone through, that
detail is the CONTENT of the act, not another task. Return one task and let it carry the detail:
  "Hand the shift log to the incoming supervisor. Tell them which orders are still open, which trucks are
   late, and what the yard still has to load."
    → ["Hand the shift log to the incoming supervisor, covering open orders, late trucks and what the yard
        still has to load"]
The same goes for the MECHANICS of doing it — sitting down together, opening the file, pulling up the
screen, walking over — and for a TECHNIQUE or rule of thumb for performing the act ("take the strong side
off first"). None of those is a task on its own; they belong to the act they serve, or they are dropped.

## A THING THE WORKER MUST KNOW IS NOT A TASK
Knowledge, awareness, judgement and being available are states, not activities: "know when to call the
supervisor", "be familiar with the delivery windows", "be aware of which machines are down". Return a task
only for what the worker DOES about it, and only if the entry actually says so.

## EVERY TASK BEGINS WITH A VERB
Never return a bare noun phrase or an "-ing" fragment. An entry that names activities as nouns still gets
proper action statements, with the minimal verb the worker's own words imply:
  "The rest of my week is status meetings, safety walkthroughs, and the parts inventory."
    → ["Hold status meetings", "Walk the site for safety", "Count the parts inventory"]

## Carry shared context into each task
If a subject, system, project, or setting scopes the whole entry, attach it to EACH task so none becomes a
stranded fragment ("confirm they are resolved" — resolved on WHAT?):
  "Escalate the pump-replacement defects and confirm they are resolved"
    → ["Escalate the pump-replacement defects", "Confirm the pump-replacement defects are resolved"]

## Drop what is not a task at all
Commentary about the interview or the list ("I forgot to mention", "Nothing else to add", "this covers
everything I do"), explanations of why, and background sentences with no activity in them contribute no
task. If an entry contains NO task, return an empty list for it.

## Phrasing
Keep the worker's words, vocabulary, and specifics. Do not invent detail, do not generalize, do not fix
grammar beyond what the split requires. Write each task as a plain action statement without "I", "my",
"we" or "our" — "Prepare and send discovery materials to defense counsel", not "I prepare ...".
An entry that is already a single task comes back as a one-item list with its text UNCHANGED.

Input (JSON array of {"id": int, "text": str}):
$tasks_json

Return ONLY a JSON array — no markdown:
[{"id": 0, "tasks": ["...", ...]}, ...]
