WHAT DIVIDES ONE TASK FROM ANOTHER

A task inventory is a taxonomy: every entry exists because something distinguishes it from every other
entry. Below are the dimensions two pieces of work can differ along, and whether a difference along each
one earns a SEPARATE ENTRY or belongs INSIDE one as an example.

ONE TEST GOVERNS ALL OF THEM. Does changing this dimension change WHAT THE WORKER DOES — the steps, the
judgement, the skill it takes? Not what the thing is called, not who is served, not when it happens. If the
work is the same work, it is one entry however unlike the two instances look.

CUT HORIZONTALLY: THE ACT IS THE ENTRY, NOT THE LARGER JOB IT SERVES. Where the same act — the same
steps, the same judgement, the same skill — recurs inside several of the occupation's jobs or workstreams,
it is ONE entry across all of them, with those jobs as its examples. Do not bury an act's instances as steps
of the larger duties they feed: for an electrician, testing circuits and recording the readings is one entry
whether the test belongs to a new installation, a fault call, or a scheduled safety check — the installation
and fault-call entries keep only the work that is theirs alone. A cut that gives each larger job its own copy
of a shared act hides the occupation's most widespread work inside vertical bundles, and the bundles then
read as overlapping. When choosing between naming the recurring act once and distributing it into the jobs it
serves, name the act.
THE HORIZONTAL CUT NEVER CROSSES A DIVIDING DIMENSION. It gathers INSTANCES of one act across jobs; it
does not gather DIFFERENT acts under one banner. The two sides of an exchange are two acts — each side is
named once across all jobs, never one entry spanning both sides; and acts split by a dividing trigger
stay split, each cut horizontally on its own.

THESE DIVIDE
  · THE ACT. Different verbs on the same object are different entries — inspecting a roof and repairing
    one, drafting a document and approving one, loading a machine and servicing it.
  · THE SIDE OF AN EXCHANGE. Issuing a request and answering one received, sending work out for another
    party's decision and deciding on work they send in, ordering supplies and filling an order that
    arrives — these are different acts EVEN WHEN THE SAME WORKERS routinely do both. Each side has its own
    trigger (the counterpart's move arrives on its own schedule) and its own product, so a shared plan —
    "handle the exchange end to end" — does not unite them.
    AN EXCHANGE NEEDS AN OPPOSING OR INDEPENDENT PARTY, one acting in its own interest: a request served
    on them, their demand answered, their submission ruled on. Question-and-answer inside the worker's own
    operation is not an exchange — answering staff questions, directing their work and unblocking their
    tasks are the upkeep of one supervisory responsibility (the CHORES bullet below).
  · WHAT THE RECEIVER MUST BE TOLD, where it changes what the worker produces. Persuading one receiver,
    seeking a second's approval before the work can proceed, informing a third after the fact — those are
    three acts, and they stay three whether the telling is written, spoken face to face, or called in over
    a radio. It is the verb that divides them, not who receives the work: reach for the act, not for the
    audience.
  · WHAT IT TAKES TO PRODUCE IT. Where two outputs demand DIFFERENT JUDGEMENT, they are two acts: an
    output that carries the worker's own analysis or diagnosis — reasoning only someone trained in the work
    could produce — and one that relays status or logs what happened are different work, however alike
    "writing things up" or "keeping records" makes them sound. The medium is not the point: a written
    report, a spoken handover and a tag hung on a machine can each carry either. Likewise, entries that
    record the WORK ITSELF divide from entries that run the workplace's own business — time, supplies,
    money owed. This is NOT the old output-function rubber stamp: two outputs taking the SAME judgement
    (two kinds of binding instrument, two kinds of filing, two sizes of the same fitting) remain one act
    with the kinds as examples.
  · THE TRIGGER, WHERE IT CHANGES THE ACT. What prompts the work can change the work. A TRIGGER THAT IS
    MERELY THE ARRIVAL OF A JOB TYPE OR CUSTOMER SEGMENT IS THE SUBJECT MATTER, NOT A TRIGGER — phrasing a
    segment as "when such jobs arrive" does not make it divide. For a maintenance technician, attending a
    machine because a fault was reported and going round the machines to a schedule are two entries: one
    starts from a complaint and ends when it is resolved, the other starts from a date and ends when the
    round is done, and the findings feed different decisions.

THESE DO NOT DIVIDE — they belong inside an entry, as its examples
  · THE COUNTERPART'S IDENTITY. Which customer, which agency, which colleague, which body — and equally
    whether the person advised is a walk-in customer, a fleet manager, or an insurer, which is usually the
    EMPLOYER differing rather than the work. The same act for a different person is the SAME entry, and
    one entry may name several counterparts in a line: explaining a repair estimate to the vehicle's owner
    and to the workshop manager is one entry, not two.
  · SUBJECT MATTER, FIELD, JOB TYPE, CUSTOMER SEGMENT. The act is unchanged by what it is applied to, and
    workers specialising in different subjects perform the same activities on them.
    AND THE TRADE'S NAME FOR THE ACT CHANGES WITH THE SUBJECT — do not be fooled by it. Every specialism
    dresses a shared act in its own vocabulary, so the same moves arrive wearing different names, each
    setting naming its own equipment, its own paperwork and its own occasions. Strip the vocabulary and ask
    what the worker DOES, move by move; where only the subject and its trade name change, the versions
    are ONE entry with each setting's subjects as its examples. An inventory that keeps every setting's
    renamed copy publishes the division of labor, not the work.
  · THE KIND OF ARTIFACT. One act of composing, checking, or handing on, applied to many kinds of product,
    is one entry with the kinds as its examples — however differently the products are named and used.
    THE OUTPUT'S FORM AND DESTINATION ARE PART OF ITS KIND: a formal written instrument and an
    outline prepared for live use, one filing form and another, a part fitted on a new build and the same
    part fitted as a replacement, are kinds of the same made thing where producing them takes the same
    judgement. Only a difference in the JUDGEMENT of producing it — different moves, not a different
    template or a different size — marks two acts.
  · THE STAGE IN A PROCESS, for the same act BEFORE THE SAME COUNTERPART. The check that opens a job and
    the one that signs it off, both reported to the same recipient, take the same moves. A stage is a
    property of the job, not of the work. But check the recipient first: if the later point in the process
    means a DIFFERENT authority applying a different standard, that is a counterpart and it divides.
  · THE TOOL, SYSTEM, SETTING, OR MEDIUM.
  · THE CHORES OF ONE ONGOING RESPONSIBILITY. Where a worker is answerable for one continuing thing — a
    helper's work, a budget they hold, a stock of supplies, the records of their own jobs — the acts its
    upkeep demands are ONE entry, even though each is articulable, has its own occasion, and would pass an
    act test taken alone: recording entries and later chasing the ones that have not resolved, directing
    the day's work and periodically appraising it, counting what is on hand and reordering what has run
    down. No one of these acts is anybody's job on its own: asked what they do, a worker names the
    responsibility — "I keep the stores", "I supervise the helpers" — and the acts are how it is
    discharged. KEEPING ONE SET OF RECORDS is the case this bullet most often decides: entering what
    happened, reviewing what is outstanding, and reconciling or reporting the totals are its chores,
    however many kinds of record the set contains and however far apart the entry and the review fall.
    TEST: write the act alone as an entry. If it reads as a duty someone could hold apart from the
    responsibility, it divides; if it reads as a chore of the responsibility, it stays inside.
    THIS DOES NOT REACH A DELIVERABLE AND ITS AFTERLIFE: producing a thing others use and maintaining it
    later are still two entries.

A TRIGGER IS NOT A STAGE. A trigger is the event that makes work start — a fault reported, a request
received, a date arriving; a stage is a position in a sequence the work runs through. A different trigger
often means a different act; a different stage almost never does.

OCCUPATIONAL HETEROGENEITY SETS THE LEVEL OF CLOSE CALLS. An occupation spans workplaces and specialisms,
and two alike-looking entries are often one act refracted through that heterogeneity. Where the difference
between them tracks the occupation's DIVISION OF LABOR — different settings each doing their variant — they
are ONE entry at the covering abstraction, the variants its examples; splitting there publishes specialisms,
not tasks. Corpus worker sets, where known, measure this: disjoint
workers say the settings divide the act and it merges up.

WHEN DIMENSIONS DISAGREE
  · THE ACT WINS over names, artifacts, and stages: a difference in what the worker does is decisive; a
    difference in what the product is called, or where it sits in a process, is not.
  · THE SIDE OF AN EXCHANGE WINS over a shared plan or phase, and over the CHORES bullet: an exchange with
    an opposing party divides even where one worker handles it end to end.
  · THE CHORES OF ONE RESPONSIBILITY WIN over the act test taken alone: acts that are all the upkeep of
    one responsibility stay one entry however cleanly each can be articulated.
